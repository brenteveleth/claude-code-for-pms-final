WHAT HAPPENED

A slower answer became a “miss”, and a miss became a lockout.

4.2 cut the ping wait from 90 to 60 seconds. Responders who answer in 60 to 90 seconds were suddenly “missed”. The scoring code treats a miss like a “no” (−0.12, against +0.08 for a yes) and never gives points back.

Low score means rarely asked. Rarely asked means no chance to earn points back. Four responders (Vesper, The Undertow, Meteor Mite, Farlight) slid to the bottom and stayed there.

Most likely cause, inferred: those four answer slowly. There is no response-time data to prove it.

13 Aug

27 Aug – 5 Sep

The release notes mention only the proximity change. The acceptance-weight cut shows up in the changelog and in config.py, not in the notes. Priya’s “seasonal dip” view does not fit an abrupt start at 09:04 on release day.

MODULE 4 · X-RAY VISION

In the code, a miss is a “no”, and nothing gives points back.

# 00-rook/code/dispatch-routing/offer.py 25 for responder in routing.rank_for_callout(callout): 26 answer = offer_to(responder, callout) 27 if answer == TAKEN: 28 history.record_accepted(responder) 29 return responder 30 history.record_declined(responder) # turned down AND missed31 return None
+0.08a ping taken
−0.12turned down, or missed
noneno decay, no reset, no credit for being asked
The prompt I wrote
what takes points off somebody, then find everything that puts them back

What it showed: only two lines change a score. Taken adds 0.08. Anything else, including “no answer before the wait ended”, takes 0.12 off. Nothing fades or resets, and a 2019 TODO about easing scores back was never done. A low rank also means rarely asked, so a quiet responder cannot earn their way out.

Source: offer.py:30, history.py, config.py. Also in the code: 4.2 cut the recent-acceptance weight from 0.40 to 0.25, which the release notes do not mention. Scores sit in memory, so whether a restart resets them is unknown.

MODULE 5 · SUPER SPEED

A two-phase fix, and a prototype Helen can click.

Phase 1 · now: stop the damage

  1. Put the ping wait back to 90 seconds
  2. Raise the score floor from 0.0 to 0.3
  3. Reset the four to 0.50, once

Phase 2 · next release: stop it coming back

  • First 2 misses in a row cost nothing, then −0.06 each
  • Log how long each answer takes

Status: proposal. Helen has not confirmed it and engineering has not reviewed it.

MODULE 6 · SIDEKICKS

I turned my brief review into a skill that scores any brief.

Six checks, same table every time

✓ pass (green) · ? unclear (yellow) · ✗ fail (red). Checks: 1 owner · 2 success measure · 3 scope match · 4 problem before fix · 5 sourced claims · 6 definition of ready.

The prompt I wrote
I want to save a habit as something I can reuse. When I review a brief before it goes any further, here's what I actually check for:
- It names who owns it
- It says how we'll know it worked
- The scope at the end matches the scope at the start
- It explains the problem before it proposes a fix

* Any assertions are backed with data and sources documented
* It includes a "definition of ready" for how we know we've met the requirements of the brief


Turn that into a skill called review-checklist, so I can point it at any brief and get the same check every time, without me explaining it again.

What it produced: .claude/skills/review-checklist/SKILL.md. Run on my own 4.2 brief it scored 2 of 6, then 3 of 6 after I added a definition of ready, a 4-week window and “replay” wording.

KEEPING IT FROM HAPPENING AGAIN

Each way 4.2 slipped through maps to a check in the skill.

What went wrong around 4.2Check

This mapping is my reading, not a measured result.

What the skill has already caught

  • My own 4.2 brief: 2 of 6 on the first run, no definition of ready and no measurement window. Fixed, now 3 of 6.
  • A classmate’s brief (2 of 6): it said missed pings rose “to 18% on release day”. The data says 12 Aug was 28.0% missed. The 18.0% is the average after 4.2.

The habit

  • A saved task runs it on the brief every Monday at about 9:06 AM and reports what changed.
  • It only reviews. It never edits the brief.

Limit: it reviews briefs. It would not have caught the miss-scoring in the code. It would have asked who owned the answer and what “done” meant.
